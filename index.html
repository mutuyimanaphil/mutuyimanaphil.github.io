PROJECT 01 Completed prototype

Smart Electric Cooker with Safety Control

Final-year dissertation project at INES-Ruhengeri. Conventional cookers are open loop: you pick a power level and hope it lands near the heat you wanted. This automated, smoke-free electric cooker closes the loop. An Arduino Uno reads a MAX6675 K-type thermocouple and an HX711 load cell, and switches an SSR-40DA solid-state relay driving a nichrome heating element. The firmware began as an on–off thermostat with three cooking modes (60 °C, 90 °C and 110 °C) and a 3 °C hysteresis band, and was then upgraded to time-proportional control for steadier temperature. A latching emergency cutoff at 200 °C prevents overheating. A Python (PySide6) dashboard shows live data over a serial link. Testing showed accurate control in all modes, and a cost comparison using RURA electricity tariffs found electric cooking competitive with, and often cheaper than, charcoal and LPG.

PROJECT 02 In progress

Heat Retention Chamber

The next stage adds a second, insulated chamber based on Runonko, a traditional Rwandan method that cooks food with retained heat. Food is brought to the boil in the active chamber, then the pot moves to the retention chamber, where thick insulation finishes the cooking with no further electricity. Slow foods like beans, which normally keep a stove running for two to three hours, would draw power for only the first 20 to 30 minutes, an estimated saving of up to 65%. Design and testing are in progress.
